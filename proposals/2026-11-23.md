## Lead (draft written): Two-Sided Markets Shaped by Platform-Guided Search

- **Authors:** Kwok Hao Lee (NUS Business School) and Leon Musolff (Wharton)
- **Journal/year:** Econometrica 94(6), November 2026, pp. 2081-2115. DOI 10.3982/ECTA22288
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA22288 (open PDF on the Econometric Society site)
- **Why it matters:** Tests the self-preferencing theory of harm against Amazon's Buy Box with proprietary seller-side data and a structural model of consumer choice, the featuring algorithm, pricing and entry. Finds that about ninety-five percent of consumers consider only the featured offer and that the algorithm favors Amazon, yet the counterfactuals say the favoritism is roughly consumer-surplus maximizing. Directly relevant to current platform cases.
- **Full text accessible:** yes, full published article, read in this run. Online supplement and replication package (Zenodo) not read.
- **Draft:** episodes/2026-11-23-amazon-buybox-platform-guided-search.md (status: draft, about 3,300 spoken words)

## Alternate 1: Hub-and-Spoke Collusion With a Third-Party Pricing Algorithm

- **Author:** Joseph E. Harrington, Jr.
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Industrial Economics 74, 2026, pp. 181-196. DOI 10.1111/joie.70017
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1111/joie.70017
- **Why it matters:** A data analytics company coordinates competitors' prices through a shared pricing algorithm, the RealPage-style fact pattern. A notable result per the abstract: the vendor's efficiency is a facilitating factor for collusion, so markets where the third party's service is grounded in genuine efficiency still deserve scrutiny.
- **Full text accessible:** only the abstract was verified in this run (via an aggregator listing). Publisher full text may be paywalled; an author-site short CPI piece on the same theme exists but is not the paper. A draft would need the full text first.

## Alternate 2: Preemptive Entry and Technology Diffusion: The Market for Drive-In Theaters

- **Authors:** Ricard Gil, Jean-Francois Houde, Shilong Sun, Yuya Takahashi
- **Journal/year:** RAND Journal of Economics 57(3), Fall 2026 (issue pp. 469-807; article page range not verified). NBER Working Paper 29408 is the open version.
- **Link:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w29408
- **Why it matters:** An empirical test of entry preemption using a testable non-monotonicity (preemption matters most in intermediate-size markets) in U.S. drive-in theaters, 1945-1957, then a dynamic entry game to quantify it. Per the RAND abstract, preemptive motives raise early entry by up to forty percent in mid-size markets, raise entry costs about five percent and lower firms' expected value about one percent. A clean example of identification from a structural prediction, and a different sub-topic from the last three episodes.
- **Full text accessible:** yes, NBER working paper (open) and an author-hosted resubmission PDF; the published RAND version was not read here.
